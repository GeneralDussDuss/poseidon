◆ SECOND BOARD // T-EMBED CC1101 PLUS

Every radio on one board. No hats.

POSEIDON now targets the LilyGO T-Embed CC1101 (Plus) alongside the Cardputer. On the Cardputer you swap a hat for each band. On the T-Embed the radios are already soldered on: CC1101 sub-GHz, an nRF24 at 2.4 GHz, a PN532 NFC reader, IR transmit and receive, plus the ESP32-S3's own WiFi and BLE. One build flag, a bigger screen, a rotary encoder, and a battery.

◐ Active bring-up. Hardware is real and verified. The port is landing feature by feature, and every status below is honest about what runs today.

01 // WHY THE T-EMBED

A self-contained deck

The Cardputer is a keyboard-first pocket computer that borrows radios from swappable hats, one at a time. The T-Embed CC1101 Plus is the opposite trade: no keyboard, but every radio POSEIDON cares about is already on the board and powered from an internal cell. Same firmware, same menus, same arsenal. A different body.

Sub-GHz, built in

The onboard CC1101 covers roughly 300 to 928 MHz with a switched antenna band-select network. Capture, analyze, and replay OOK remotes with no Hydra hat to seat.

CC1101

2.4 GHz nRF24

The Plus SKU populates an nRF24L01+ for promiscuous ESB and HID work, the MouseJack surface, and nRF-side BLE spam. Same driver POSEIDON already ships.

nRF24 · PLUS

PN532 NFC

The T-Embed exclusive. A full 13.56 MHz NFC reader on I2C that the Cardputer simply does not have. Read tags, identify the family, dump Mifare Classic sectors.

EXCLUSIVE

IR transmit and receive

An IR LED on GPIO2 and a receiver on GPIO1, both onboard. Learn a remote, then replay it. No cap swap, no external emitter.

IR TX + RX

Runs on battery

A BQ25896 charger and a BQ27220 fuel gauge over I2C mean real runtime and a real percentage on screen. Unplug and keep working.

POWER

Encoder and LED ring

A rotary encoder with SELECT and BACK, wrapped in an eight pixel WS2812B ring. A dial instead of a keyboard, and light that reacts to what the radios are doing.

INPUT
★ THE T-EMBED EXCLUSIVE · NFC

A card reader the Cardputer can never have

The board carries a PN532 on its own I2C bus. POSEIDON drives it with a self-contained reader, no external NFC library, on a separate Wire bus so it never fights anything else the chip is doing. Tap a tag and you get the UID, the ATQA and SAK bytes, and the tag family named on screen. On a Mifare Classic card, one press walks the sectors with the common default keys and writes the dump to the SD card.

◐ WRITTEN FROM THE PN532 SPEC · PENDING ON-HARDWARE VALIDATION

What the reader does, and where it stands

The driver is complete and reads cleanly on paper: framing, checksums, InListPassiveTarget, MIFARE auth and read all follow the PN532 manual. It has not yet been proven against a live tag on the metal. Until it is, it wears a work in progress badge, the same rule the rest of the site follows.

$ nfc read · iso14443a passive target
tag family from SAK ........ NAMED  UID + ATQA + SAK ... SHOWN
mifare classic sector dump ...... CODED  8 default keys, re-select on auth fail
dump target ....... /poseidon/nfc/<uid>.mfd on SD
on-card validation ............. PENDING

Identify any 14443A tag

UID of any length, ATQA and SAK on screen, and the family resolved from SAK: NTAG or Ultralight, Mifare Classic 1K or 4K, Plus, DESFire, JCOP, or plain ISO14443-4.

READ

Dump Mifare Classic

Press once on a Classic card and it authenticates each sector with eight common keys, re-selecting the tag after any failed auth, then saves every block it recovered as a raw .mfd file.

DUMP

Own bus, no collisions

The PN532 sits on Wire1 at address 0x24 with its own reset line, kept off the main I2C bus so it never interferes with the fuel gauge, the charger, or anything else the board brings up.

I2C

Lives in src/nfc_hw.* and src/features/nfc_read.cpp, board-gated so it compiles to inert stubs on the Cardputer. Read your own cards only.

▲ TWO REAL CAVEATS, STATED UP FRONT

The nRF24's chip-select pins are triple-booked with UART0 and the external header, so serial logging and 2.4 GHz traffic can interfere, and LilyGO's own issue tracker notes some Plus units lose nRF range over time. And because the SD card, the CC1101 and the nRF24 all live on one SPI bus, the SD mount has to cooperate with the display's use of that same bus. That coordination is exactly what the bring-up work below is closing out.

Neither board wins outright. The Cardputer keeps the keyboard and reaches LoRa, GNSS and wired Ethernet through hats. The T-Embed folds sub-GHz, 2.4 GHz, NFC and IR onto one board and adds a battery gauge. Pick the body the mission wants.

04 // PORT STATUS

What runs today

The honest ledger. Green is proven on the board or shares a code path that is. Amber is in active bring-up, written but not yet signed off on this hardware. No feature is claimed as done before it is.

● READY

Core firmware and display

The POSEIDON build compiles for this board behind its own flag. The ST7789 panel comes up landscape through the LovyanGFX path, with the panel reset and power gate handled per LilyGO's own sources.

● READY

Menu and encoder

The full menu tree renders on the wider screen, driven by the rotary encoder and the two buttons in place of the keyboard. The NFC entry is wired into the root menu on this board only.

● READY

Sub-GHz CC1101 driver

The CC1101 init, strict chip detection and stable RSSI reads are board-gated to the T-Embed's own SPI pins. The chip is verified by PARTNUM and VERSION before any capture is trusted.

◐ BRING-UP

NFC reader

The PN532 driver and the read plus Mifare dump feature are written to spec and compile, but have not yet been proven against a live tag on the metal. Labelled work in progress until they are.

◐ BRING-UP

Sub-GHz spectrum view

The waterfall and spectrum buffers were sized to the Cardputer's narrower screen and overran on the T-Embed's 320 pixel width. The fix is in the tree, sizing every buffer to the board's real width, pending a compile and an on-device pass.

◐ BRING-UP

SD card mount

The SD card, the CC1101 and the nRF24 all share one SPI bus with the display. The pins are corrected for this board, and the remaining work is making the SD mount cooperate with the display's lock on that bus rather than seizing it.

This ledger moves as the board is exercised. When a card turns green here it has run on real hardware, not just compiled.
